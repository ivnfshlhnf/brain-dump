## Design Specificity Verdict

**Split — and it fails in the half you live in.**

**LLM assessment:** The Note card is unmistakably this product. Whole-Note-before-commit, the ember eyebrow stating the pending decision, the burning edge, the flip to teal with the vault path in its place — the state of a Note is readable from a 200px crop with no text legible. That is a design system, not a palette.

The three surfaces you actually use are generic. An empty textarea with one button; a question box with one button; eleven undifferentiated settings fields. Swap the wordmark and this is any Svelte starter shell.

And the one fact no competitor can copy — that these Notes land in your **real Obsidian vault** — is invisible. The word "Obsidian" appears nowhere in the UI. The saved path is inert text. Related wikilinks are `<li>{link}</li>`, plain strings you cannot follow. Ask citations are inert too. The entire positioning is rendered as unclickable monospace.

## Overall Impression

The engineering underneath this is honest and the one signature component is genuinely good. What's wrong is that the app spends 95% of its life on screens that never show it, and the two moments that carry all the meaning — the press, and the commit — are the two moments with the least design on them. The single biggest opportunity is not more colour or more density; it is giving weight to the instant a thought becomes permanent.

## What's Working

1. **The Note is shown as a Note, not a receipt for one.** Title, tags, category, full body, summary, key points and Related in one card, serif at 1rem/1.55 inside a 34rem measure. It reads like a page. This is what stops the app being a CRUD form.
2. **The two-ink state system survives contact with pixels.** Ember before commit, teal after — colour doing semantic work, correct in both schemes, state readable without reading a word. Very few systems this small hold a rule this cleanly.
3. **The failure copy is better than the product it sits in.** "Save failed — Dump kept: {message}". The queue-read error refusing to report a reassuring zero. Per-service health rows so a failure points at one field. Honest engineering made visible.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] On the append path, the countdown is a lie
**Why it matters:** `.burn` renders for every session (`App.svelte:436`) and animates full-to-zero over five seconds, but `saveAndFinalize` returns early on an unconfirmed append (`:183`). The bar empties and **nothing is saved**. "Save now" makes it worse: `flush()` clears the timer, then no-ops — a dead button that also permanently stops the clock. The card *is* the clock; on this path the signature component's signature behaviour makes a promise it does not keep, at the exact moment the user is deciding whether their thought is safe.
**Fix:** Animate `.burn` only when the session will actually autosave. On an unconfirmed append, render a static ember edge and state the truth where the eyebrow is — "waiting for you: Append, or Save as new Note". Make "Save now" either confirm the append or say why it can't.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P0] The moment of capture is neither fast nor protected
**Why it matters:** Three measured facts: `document.activeElement` is `BODY` on load (no autofocus); `Meta+Enter` does nothing (no keyboard commit anywhere in the app); and `text` is a plain component variable with no persistence, so everything typed before the Capture press exists only in volatile memory. PRODUCT.md says *"Failure is any friction at the moment of capture. Everything else is negotiable; that is not."* The app charges one tap to focus plus one tap on a 90×35px target, and the "thought survives" guarantee doesn't begin until the second tap. There is a hole in the chain at the exact point the product exists to protect.
**Fix:** Autofocus the dump field on mount when there's no session; bind Cmd/Ctrl+Enter to capture; persist `text` to localStorage on input and restore on mount; raise the primary control to a 44px minimum height.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable optimize`

### [P1] In-flight is visually identical to empty
**Why it matters:** During the cloud round-trip the only change is the textarea greying and the primary button dropping to `opacity: 0.45` — the same treatment that means "nothing typed yet". The disabled primary's label measures **1.33:1** against its own fill in light mode. Ask already solves this ("Reading your vault…"); Capture does not. There is also no `aria-live` region anywhere, so the status line — the app's only feedback channel — is never announced.
**Fix:** Give Capture an in-flight label matching Ask. Give `:disabled` its own colour treatment instead of a blanket fade — DESIGN.md already names the fade a system-level defect. Wrap `.status` in `aria-live="polite"`.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P1] The signature surface has no material
**Why it matters:** `--raised` vs `--ground` measures **1.09:1** light, **1.11:1** dark; `--rule` vs `--ground` is 1.31:1. Zero `box-shadow`, zero `transition` declarations. The Note — the object the entire product exists to produce — is a white rectangle on a near-white rectangle separated by a hairline you can barely see, and pressing any control produces no visible change at all. This is the gap DESIGN.md states deliberately, and it is the substance of "bare and not modern".
**Fix:** Implement the committed tactility direction — two-layer ambient shadow on `.note` and `.answer`, a second larger radius step for cards distinct from controls, 120–160ms transitions on hover/focus/press for every interactive element, a real press depression. All under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable bolder`

### [P1] The end of the journey is the smallest type on the page
**Why it matters:** `Saved Note: <title>` renders at 11px `--dim` at the bottom of a ~2,400px document on a phone, after the teal edge that carries the meaning has scrolled off screen. The saved vault path is inert text; Related wikilinks and Ask citations are inert text. Peak-end says the last frame is the one that's remembered, and this one whispers. It also strands the positioning: the Note is now in a real Obsidian vault and the app gives you no door into it.
**Fix:** Promote the commit onto the card itself (teal edge plus a "filed" line where the eyebrow was), scroll the card into view on save, and render the saved path and every Related link as `obsidian://open?vault=…&file=…` links.
**Suggested command:** `/impeccable shape`

## Persona Red Flags

**Casey (Distracted Mobile User)** — the exact user this product was built for, and the one it fails hardest. Every primary action sits at the top or far bottom, never the thumb zone: on the review screen `Append` is ~1,900px down, `Save now` past 2,400px. Touch targets measured below 44×44 across the board — nav tabs 27px tall, `ask` **25px wide**, primary button 90×35px. No autofocus, so walking one-handed you must land a tap on a box before the first character. No draft persistence: interrupted mid-typing, tab discarded, the thought is gone before it ever became a Dump.

**Alex (Impatient Power User)** — no keyboard accelerator exists anywhere; `Meta+Enter` verified dead. Tab order routes through all three nav buttons before reaching the capture field, on every load, forever. "Save now" is dead on the append path — the one control an impatient user reaches for first. No way to skip the review.

**Sam (Accessibility-Dependent User)** — zero `aria-live` regions, so no save confirmation, failure, or config rejection is ever announced. Tabs are bare `<button>`s in a `<nav>` with no `role="tablist"` and `aria-selected` verified `null` on all three. Zero headings in the default document — no `h1` anywhere, the Note title is an orphan `h2`. The primary button label measures **4.15:1** against its ember fill in light mode, below AA for 13px text, on the most important control in the product. The five-second window is a time-limited action with no extension.

**The Author-Operator (project-specific: you, daily)** — the app never links back into Obsidian, so every successful save ends in a manual hunt. The **diagnostics panel is the most information-dense surface in the product while the Note card is the least** — the tooling outweighs the artifact. Your own carefully-written error messages land in the smallest, faintest type on the page. `Case-sensitive` and `Embeddings database` are fine for you today and opaque in eight months.

**The Returner (project-specific: months later, asking a question)** — Ask is the emptiest screen in the app: no history, no example of a question that works. **Citations are unclickable** — the answer names the Notes it drew from and then refuses to open any of them, which is the entire point of the feature. No indication that asking re-embeds the vault and costs money.

## Minor Observations

- **Config is a wall.** 11 identically-weighted fields, ordered so Managed folder and Embeddings database interrupt the CouchDB block before the LLM block starts. The `Case-sensitive` checkbox is stretched to **358px × 13px** by the global `input { width: 100% }` rule. The "spends LLM credit" warning is 11px dim mono with no alarm colour and no confirm.
- The native resize grabber is visible on **all three** textareas, not just the Dump that DESIGN.md flags.
- `.eyebrow` uppercases a user's Note title: `append to "Offline capture and the outbox"` renders as `APPEND TO "OFFLINE CAPTURE AND THE OUTBOX"` — a person's words in the machine's hand, casing destroyed.
- Tags are spaced with `join('  ')` — a double-space text hack standing in for layout.
- `"What did I think about..."` uses three periods, not an ellipsis. `"What are you thinking?"` is the best sentence in the product.
- `theme-color` is hardcoded `#f0f6fa`, the light ground only — dark-mode browser chrome is wrong.
- Desktop composition: content occupies the top ~24% of a 1280×900 viewport with no anchor at the bottom.

## Questions to Consider

1. The Dump reaches CouchDB before anything else — that's Principle 1 and genuinely rare. Why is that guarantee the *quietest* thing in the interface, an 11px hint that only appears after the review screen opens?
2. The clock is drawn on the top edge of the card. Your thumb is in the Context field at the bottom of a 2,400px page. Should the countdown be attached to the card at all — or to the thing you're actually touching?
3. If a Note is a page in a notebook, why can't you cross anything out? Title, tags and category are unowned. Is "add context" a design, or a workaround for not having built editing?
4. The app writes into a real Obsidian vault and never says the word. Does the positioning survive being invisible?
5. Three tabs for a product used in bursts of seconds: how often is `config` opened after week one? Is `ask` — rarer, slower, more expensive — really a sibling of `capture`?
6. **You say it looks bare. Is bare the problem — or is it that the only screen you ever see is the one with the least on it?** The Note card is good. The app spends 95% of its life on a screen that never shows one.
